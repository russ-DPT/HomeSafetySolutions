Getting Started

What does Home Safety Solutions do?

We help older adults and people with disabilities live safely at home. A Doctor of Physical Therapy looks at the person, their daily routines, and their home, then gives you a clear, prioritized plan: what to change, what equipment or technology to use, and who can do the work.

Who is this for?

Older adults who want to stay in their own home, people coming home from a hospital or rehab stay, families caring for someone nearby or from a distance, and anyone planning a move or a remodel with safety in mind.

How is this different from a regular home safety checklist?

A checklist looks at the house. We look at the person in the house: their strength, balance, vision, memory, and daily routines. Our recommendations fit how that person actually moves and lives.

How do I get started?

Book a $50 Initial Consultation. It's a 30-minute call where we talk through your goals and concerns and recommend the right service. The $50 is credited toward any visit you book within 30 days.

Which service should I choose?

Most people start with a Home Safety Visit. Coming home from a hospital? Choose Ready for Discharge. Supporting someone from a distance? Choose a Remote Caregiving Plan. Many families combine services on one visit to save. Not sure? The Initial Consultation will point you to the right fit.

Do I need a doctor's referral?

No. Our services are home safety and technology consulting, so you can book any service directly. With your written permission, we can send a copy of your report to your doctor, and we encourage it. If we notice something that needs medical attention, we will let you know and recommend that you contact your doctor.

Is this physical therapy treatment?

No. Our services are home safety and technology consulting. We do not provide hands-on physical therapy treatment. If treatment would help, we will tell you and recommend that you talk with your doctor.

Reimbursement and Insurance

Is this covered by insurance?

Usually not. Medicare and most health plans pay to treat medical conditions, not to make a home safer. Medicare does not cover home modifications or items like grab bars, and it does not cover home technology like smart locks or thermostats. Home Safety Solutions is a private-pay consulting service and does not bill Medicare or insurance. Veterans: the VA may pay for items Medicare does not cover. See the Veterans section below.

Why doesn't Medicare cover home safety?

Medicare covers equipment that serves a mainly medical purpose, like wheelchairs, oxygen, and hospital beds. It considers items like grab bars, bath seats, and raised toilet seats to be for comfort or convenience, even though they help prevent falls. Home modifications and home technology fall outside Medicare coverage for the same reason.

Can I submit it to my insurance myself?

Yes, you can try. We provide an itemized receipt on request. We cannot guarantee reimbursement.

Does Medicare Advantage cover any of this?

Some Medicare Advantage plans offer extra benefits for home safety items, bathroom safety devices, or in-home support. Call the number on your member card and ask about "home safety" or "bathroom safety" benefits.

Will long-term care insurance pay?

Some long-term care policies reimburse home modifications or care coordination. Check your policy or call your insurer, and submit your itemized receipt.

Does Florida Medicaid cover home modifications?

Members of Florida Medicaid's long-term care program may be able to get home accessibility adaptations and emergency response systems. Ask your case manager.

Can I use my HSA or FSA?

Possibly. Some home safety costs may qualify with a letter of medical necessity. Check with your plan administrator.

Are home safety costs tax deductible?

Some home modifications made for medical reasons may count as medical expenses on your taxes. Check with your tax advisor.

Is financial help available for home modifications?

Some local agencies and nonprofits help with home modification costs. Your local Area Agency on Aging is a good place to start. We are happy to point you in the right direction during your consultation.

Veterans

I'm a veteran. Will the VA pay for this?

It may. The Department of Veterans Affairs may pay for home safety items and home modifications that Medicare does not cover, such as grab bars, bathroom safety equipment, and accessibility changes, for veterans enrolled in VA health care when a VA clinician finds them medically needed. When VA payment may be possible, Dr. L'HommeDieu will make every attempt to refer you to the VA first.

I'm not enrolled in VA health care. What should I do?

Enrolling in VA health care is a good first step. Once enrolled, ask your VA primary care team about a home safety evaluation.

Do you offer anything for veterans at no cost?

Yes. Veterans enrolled in VA health care can schedule a free phone call to understand what the VA may provide and how to ask for it. Call (813) 867-3372 or email info@homesafety.solutions.

Why would you send me to the VA instead of doing the work yourselves?

Dr. L'HommeDieu served for many years as a VA Home Safety Coordinator. If the VA can pay for what you need, you should use that benefit first. We will help you understand how.

Can you send my report to my VA care team?

Yes, with your written permission. Sharing it can help your VA team decide what the VA can provide.

Home Safety Visits

What happens during a Home Safety Visit?

Dr. L'HommeDieu walks through the home with you, watches how daily tasks are done (like getting in and out of the shower, on and off the toilet, and up and down steps), checks lighting, flooring, entrances, and stairs, and takes photos of each concern.

How long does a visit take?

60 to 120 minutes, depending on the home and the person's needs. Visits with add-ons may take longer.

What do I receive?

A written photo report with a prioritized list of hazards and clear recommendations for each one. You will receive it within 3 business days after your visit. With your written permission, we can also send a copy to your doctor, therapist, or case manager.

Should family members be there?

Yes, if possible. The person who lives in the home should be present, and anyone who helps with care is welcome, in person or by phone or video.

Do you install anything?

No. We assess and recommend. You choose who does the work. This keeps our advice independent.

I need a ramp. How long does it need to be?

The ADA Standards for Accessible Design call for a maximum slope of 1:12 for ramps, meaning every 1 inch of vertical rise needs at least 12 inches of ramp length. So a 24-inch rise (a typical set of steps) needs a ramp about 24 feet long, plus level landings at the top and bottom. Steeper ramps can be unsafe, especially for wheelchairs and walkers.

To picture the length for your own entry, this free tool lets you enter your rise and see the ramp length and slope: ramp.engineer. Bring your numbers to your Home Safety Visit and we can plan placement, landings, and turns together.

What is a Right Home Assessment?

A visit to help you decide whether a home will work long term, whether it's your current home or one you are thinking of buying or renting. It is $250.

What is a Build Verify Visit?

After the work is done, we come back and check that it matches the specifications, such as grab bar height and placement. It is $100.

Can it be done virtually?

What if discharge is only a few days away?

What if the discharge date changes?

Remote Caregiving

What is a Remote Caregiving Plan?

A complete, room-by-room plan for the technology that lets family support a loved one from a distance: fall detection, safe entry, video check-ins, lighting, stove and thermostat safety, and storm backup. A qualified installer carries it out, and we train your family to use it.

What does it cost?

$250 in-home, $175 by video, or $100 added to another visit ($50 if it is your second add-on). A yearly Plan Review is $150.

Does it include a full home hazard check?

The Remote Caregiving Plan focuses on technology. For a full home hazard check too, add a Home Safety Visit: both on the same trip are $350.

What do I receive?

A written room-by-room plan, an Installer Prescription your installer can quote from, an Alert and Response Plan, a Storm and Outage Plan, a Home Monitoring Agreement, one family training session by video, and one follow-up call after installation.

Do you monitor my parent?

No. We design the system and train your family. Alerts go to the people and services you choose. We do not provide 24-hour monitoring or emergency response. In an emergency, call 911.

What if my parent does not want to be monitored?

We start with their goals and concerns. The plan includes only what the person agrees to, or what their legal representative approves if they cannot decide for themselves. Every plan includes a written Home Monitoring Agreement.

Do you put cameras in the home?

Only where the person agrees. We do not recommend cameras in bedrooms or bathrooms. Many fall detection options work without cameras.

Do I have to buy certain brands?

No. We write specifications, so you can buy anywhere. We may name examples that meet the specifications, and we are never paid by the companies that make them.

Who installs the devices?

Family can often set up plug-in devices with our guidance by video. Hardwired items go to qualified installers, such as licensed electricians, security and low-voltage installers, and HVAC contractors. Gas ranges go to licensed gas contractors. You choose and pay the installer directly.

Will it work during a hurricane?

Every plan includes a Storm and Outage Plan that shows what keeps working without power or internet and what the backup plan is.

I live out of state. Can you still help?

Yes. The home must be in Florida, but family members can join the assessment and training by video from anywhere.

How is this different from an Assistive Technology Consultation?

An Assistive Technology Consultation matches equipment and technology to the person. A Remote Caregiving Plan designs the whole system that lets family support that person from a distance, including alerts, home access, training, and outage planning. Many families book both on one visit.

What happens when needs change?

Book a Remote Caregiving Plan Review ($150) yearly or after a change in health. It is included in Membership.

Contractors and Installers

Do you do the construction or installation?

No. We assess and write the specifications. A licensed contractor or qualified installer of your choice does the work.

Why don't you do the work yourselves?

When the same company assesses and builds, there's a reason to recommend more work. Keeping them separate means our recommendations are based only on what the person needs.

Do you get paid by contractors?

No. No referral fees. No commissions. No payments from contractors, installers, suppliers, equipment companies, or device makers.

Can you recommend a contractor?

Yes. See our Contractor Network. You are always free to choose your own.

Contractor Network

What is a Contractor Consultation?

For $150, we prepare a contractor-ready prescription with room-by-room specifications and have one call with your contractor to answer questions before they quote. It is included for technology installers in every Remote Caregiving Plan.

What should I look for in a contractor?

A Florida license for the work, proof of insurance, required permits, a written quote for each item, and willingness to follow written specifications.
