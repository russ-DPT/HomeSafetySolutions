# How can I check on a parent's safety from another state?

*By Russ L'HommeDieu, DPT, ATP | Home Safety Solutions*

Combine three things: regular video visits where you actually look at the house, a small amount of technology your parent agrees to, and a local team you can call. No sensor replaces a neighbor with a key. But the right mix can tell you early when something is changing, and get help there fast when something goes wrong.

## You are not alone

AARP estimates that 63 million Americans are family caregivers, up nearly 50 percent since 2015. More than 1 in 10 live an hour or more away from the person they care for. The share of caregivers using remote monitoring rose from 13 percent in 2020 to 25 percent in 2025.

## 1. Make video visits work harder

A regular video call can be a quick safety check if you know what to look for. Ask your parent to walk you around with the phone once a month.

- **Kitchen:** fresh food in the refrigerator, expired items, burned pots, and whether the stove is used
- **Bathroom:** a bath mat that slides, towel bars used as handholds, and signs that bathing has stopped
- **Paths:** new clutter, cords or throw rugs, and furniture your parent grabs while walking
- **Mail and pills:** unopened bills and medications that are not going down as they should
- **Your parent:** new bruises, weight change, shuffling, holding the walls, or changes in mood or memory

If you see changes between visits, that is the time to act, not after a fall.

## 2. Choose technology with your parent, not for them

| Tool | What it does well | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medical alert pendant or watch | Lets your parent call for help with one button; some detect falls | Only works if worn, including in the shower and at night |
| Automatic fall detection | Can call for help when your parent cannot | Accuracy varies; real-world testing in frail older adults is limited |
| Motion and door sensors | Show routine without cameras: up in the morning, fridge opened, front door used | Show that something changed, not what happened |
| Smart speakers and video doorbells | Voice calling, reminders, seeing who comes to the door | Need Wi-Fi and some setup |
| Medication dispensers | Timed reminders and alerts for missed doses | Need someone to refill them |

A review of fall detection studies found high accuracy in some lab settings, but most of the underlying reviews were low quality. Treat fall detection as a backup, not a guarantee.

Privacy matters. In one study, adult children were far more comfortable with cameras in their mother's home than the mothers were. Seven of 10 children accepted cameras, but only 1 of 10 mothers did. Start with what your parent is comfortable with, and explain what you will and will not see.

## 3. Build a local team

Write down who can be at the house within an hour. Share the list with everyone on it.

- A neighbor or friend with a key
- The primary doctor and pharmacy
- A local home care agency, if paid help is in place
- The Area Agency on Aging, for services and benefits
- A geriatric care manager, if you need a professional to coordinate care. Care managers typically charge $75 to $300 an hour.
