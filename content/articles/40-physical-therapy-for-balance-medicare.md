# Physical therapy for balance: what it is and whether Medicare covers it

*By Russ L'HommeDieu, DPT, ATP | Home Safety Solutions*

A physical therapist (PT) tests your strength, walking, and balance, finds what is driving your fall risk, and builds an exercise plan to fix it. Medicare Part B can cover physical therapy for balance, gait, and strength, subject to coinsurance. If you are homebound, Medicare home health can bring therapy to your door.

## Who should see a PT for balance

You may benefit from physical therapy if you:

- Fell in the past year, especially more than once or with an injury
- Feel unsteady when standing or walking
- Steady yourself on furniture as you move around the house
- Need to push with your hands to get up from a chair, or have trouble stepping up a curb
- Just came home from the hospital (the month after discharge is a high-risk time)
- Have dizziness or vertigo, Parkinson disease, a past stroke, or another condition that affects balance

You do not always need a full workup first. The 2025 American Geriatrics Society recommendations say a person who feels unsteady and walks by holding furniture can be referred straight to physical therapy.

## What happens at the first visit

Your PT will ask about falls, near misses, medicines, and what you want to be able to do. Then come a few simple tests, often including:

- **Timed Up and Go:** stand up from a chair, walk 10 feet, turn, walk back, and sit. Taking 12 seconds or more signals higher risk.
- **30-Second Chair Stand:** how many times you can stand up from a chair in 30 seconds with your arms crossed.
- **4-Stage Balance Test:** holding four standing positions, from feet side by side to one foot.
- **Walking speed:** sometimes called the sixth vital sign.

Just as important, the PT watches how you walk and turn. These results give you a starting point to measure progress.

## What treatment looks like

Your plan is built around what the tests find. It may include:

- **Strength and balance training** that gets harder as you improve, often with a home program such as Otago.
- **Walker or cane fitting and training,** including turns, doorways, curbs, and getting in and out of chairs.
- **Getting up from the floor,** practiced step by step so you know what to do after a fall.
- **Vestibular therapy** for dizziness from the inner ear, such as BPPV.
- **Walking while doing a second task,** like talking or carrying something.

To prevent falls, exercise needs enough dose: at least 50 hours over 3 to 6 months, three or more times a week, and then continued. Expect your PT to give you homework and to keep you going after formal visits end.

## Does Medicare cover it?

Coverage rules change every year, so confirm with your plan. In general:

- **Outpatient therapy (Part B):** physical and occupational therapy for balance, gait, strength, and home safety training can be covered under Part B, subject to coinsurance.
- **Home health:** if you are homebound, Medicare home health includes PT, OT, and nursing that address fall risk.
- **Annual Wellness Visit:** includes fall risk screening with no cost sharing. It is a good time to ask your doctor about a PT referral. See [Medicare yearly wellness visits](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/yearly-wellness-visits).
- **Medicare Advantage:** plans follow their own rules for therapy and may add fitness benefits. Call your plan.
- **Walkers and canes:** often covered as durable medical equipment when ordered by a clinician and supplied by a Medicare-enrolled supplier, usually with about 20% coinsurance after the Part B deductible. See [Medicare DME coverage](https://medicare.gov/coverage/durable-medical-equipment-dme-coverage).
- **Grab bars and home changes:** bathroom safety items and home modifications are generally not covered by Original Medicare.

## Finding the right PT

Use the American Physical Therapy Association's [Find a PT directory](https://choosept.com/find-a-pt). When you call, ask:

- Do you specialize in balance and fall prevention?
- Do you treat dizziness and vertigo?
- Do you offer home visits?
- Will you give me a home exercise program?
