# Medicare GUIDE and caregiver pay programs: a 2026 state-by-state guide for families

*By Russ L'HommeDieu, DPT, ATP, CFPS | Home Safety Solutions*

If you care for a parent or spouse with dementia, you have probably seen posts promising that "Medicare will now help." Part of that is true. Medicare's GUIDE program is real, costs eligible families nothing, and gives you a care navigator, a 24/7 support line, caregiver training and, for some families, paid respite. But Original Medicare does not pay family members to provide care. That money, when it exists, comes from the VA, Medicaid, state aging programs and the tax code.

This guide explains what GUIDE actually offers under the 2026 rules, who qualifies for respite, how to enroll, and every other way a family caregiver can get paid, reimbursed or relieved. Florida is the worked example, and a separate section shows what changes in other states.

## Fact check: what the GUIDE posts get right and wrong

Most online posts about GUIDE are mostly accurate. They tend to leave out the 2026 rule changes that decide who actually gets the respite money, and claims about which companies are "approved partners" can only be partly checked in public records.

| What the post says | Verdict | What the official CMS rules say |
|---|---|---|
| Medicare has a program called GUIDE for dementia families | Accurate | GUIDE (Guiding an Improved Dementia Experience) is a CMS Innovation Center model that began July 1, 2024 and runs through June 30, 2032. It is a voluntary test, offered only through participating organizations in their own service areas. |
| You get a care navigator who coordinates with her doctors and is your one point of contact | Accurate, with a limit | Every participant must assign a care navigator who connects the patient and caregiver to clinical and community services. GUIDE does not replace her doctors; she keeps the right to see any provider who accepts Medicare. |
| You get a 24/7 support line from the start | Accurate once enrolled | Participants must run a round-the-clock support line. Services start after an in-person assessment (including a home visit) and CMS confirmation of eligibility, which CMS returns within 15 business days of the submission. |
| Families further along get respite care | Accurate, but narrower than implied | Respite is paid only for people with moderate or severe dementia who have an unpaid primary caregiver and live in a private home. The cap is $2,625 per program year (July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027). |
| Eligibility: dementia diagnosis, Original Medicare A and B, at home or approved community setting, not in hospice or a long-term nursing home | Accurate but incomplete | Medicare must also be the primary payer, she cannot be in PACE, and she must live in the participant's ZIP code service area. Memory care units are not eligible. Assisted living qualifies only if that facility is a CMS-approved partner of the participant. |
| No cost is mentioned | Good news the post omits | GUIDE waives the usual 20% Part B coinsurance for both the monthly care management and the respite services. Participants may not bill families for either. |
| "Tendercare is an Approved GUIDE Program Partner" | Partly verifiable | Tendercare's help center says it is a CMS-approved partner of Lifetime Redesign OT, PLLC (doing business as Vela Health), and independent GUIDE directories list that company as a participant. CMS publishes participants but not their partners, so the partner approval itself cannot be confirmed publicly. Your paperwork may name Vela Health rather than tendercare. Before enrolling, confirm the participant serves your ZIP code. |

**The rule of thumb:** any referral service may be legitimate, but always find out which CMS-listed GUIDE participant actually holds the contract, and confirm it serves your ZIP code.

## What GUIDE is and what you actually get

GUIDE pays a participating dementia care program a monthly fee to manage the whole picture for one person with dementia and the family member who cares for them. CMS reports 292 participating organizations nationwide as of late October 2026.

Every GUIDE program must provide:

- **An interdisciplinary care team** that includes, at minimum, a care navigator and a clinician experienced in dementia care.
- **An initial comprehensive assessment**, including a home visit, covering memory, daily function, behavior, medical needs, dementia stage and caregiver strain. It is repeated yearly.
- **A written, person-centered care plan** built from that assessment.
- **Care navigation**: help getting to clinical services and to community supports such as meals and transportation.
- **24/7 access to a support line** for urgent questions.
- **Caregiver training and support**, adjusted as the caregiver's needs change.
- **Screening for practical needs** (food, transportation, finances) with referrals to local agencies.
- **Respite services** for eligible families (see the respite section below).

Three rules families should know before enrolling:

1. **She keeps her own doctors.** Enrolling does not change her Medicare benefits or limit which Medicare providers she can see.
2. **One GUIDE program at a time.** She can be enrolled with only one participant per month, but can switch programs at any time by telling the current one. Any unused respite balance moves with her.
3. **Contact frequency depends on need.** Families in the higher-need tiers must be contacted at least monthly (twice monthly for people with dementia who have no caregiver). Families in the lowest tier are contacted at least quarterly.

## Who qualifies under the 2026 rules

She must meet every item below. CMS checks eligibility itself after the GUIDE program submits the assessment.

- Has dementia at any stage (mild, moderate or severe), attested by a dementia-qualified clinician on the program's roster. Mild cognitive impairment alone does not qualify.
- Enrolled in both Medicare Part A and Part B.
- Not in Medicare Advantage, a Special Needs Plan or PACE.
- Medicare is her primary insurance.
- Not enrolled in the Medicare hospice benefit.
- Not a long-term nursing home resident (CMS defines this as a stay of 101 days or more).
- Not already enrolled with another GUIDE program.
- Lives in the program's ZIP code service area.
- Lives in a private home, or in a residential care community (such as assisted living or an adult family care home) that CMS has approved as that program's partner.

**What changed on July 1, 2026.** Memory care units are no longer eligible settings; a person who moves into one is disenrolled after a 15-day transition period. Assisted living residents can stay in GUIDE only if their facility signs a CMS-approved partnership agreement with the program. All residents of assisted living and similar communities now sit in a separate tier that does not include respite.

**Model tiers decide respite.** The program scores dementia stage (Clinical Dementia Rating or FAST scale) and caregiver strain (the 22-item Zarit Burden Interview). CMS uses those scores to assign one of six tiers:

| Living situation | Tier | Dementia stage | Caregiver strain | Respite paid? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Private home, with unpaid caregiver | Low complexity dyad | Mild | Any | No |
| Private home, with unpaid caregiver | Moderate complexity dyad | Moderate or severe | Low to moderate | Yes |
| Private home, with unpaid caregiver | High complexity dyad | Moderate or severe | High | Yes |
| Private home, no caregiver | Low complexity individual | Mild | None listed | No |
| Private home, no caregiver | Moderate to high complexity individual | Moderate or severe | None listed | No |
| Approved residential care community | RCC tier | Any | Any | No |

A reassessment can move her to a new tier: every 180 days for a change in dementia stage or caregiver status, and every 30 days for a change in where she lives. If her memory or daily function has clearly declined, or your own strain has grown, ask the program for a reassessment. It may be what unlocks respite.

## The respite benefit: how much and how it works

Eligible families get up to $2,625 of respite per program year (July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027), at no cost to them. That buys roughly 76 hours of in-home help, or 25 adult day center days, or 8 days of round-the-clock facility care, or a mix.

| Setting | Billing unit | 2026 base rate | Cap alone buys about |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-home respite (must be offered by every program) | 4 hours | $138 | 19 visits (76 hours) |
| Adult day center, medical or social (optional) | 1 day | $104 | 25 days |
| 24-hour facility stay (optional) | 24 hours | $321 | 8 days |

Rules that matter when you plan a break:

- **Who qualifies:** only the moderate and high complexity dyad tiers, meaning moderate or severe dementia plus a named unpaid caregiver, living in a private home.
- **Ask early which settings your program offers.** In-home respite is required; adult day and facility respite are at the program's option.
- **The cap resets every July 1.** Unused money does not carry over. A person who enrolls mid-year still gets the full annual amount, so plan to use it before June 30.
- **No surprise bills.** The program may not charge you for respite up to the cap or bill you for the difference. You can choose to pay privately for more.
- **Medicaid families:** GUIDE respite is meant to add to Medicaid respite, not replace it. The program must coordinate with her Medicaid plan so the same hours are not billed twice.
- **Switching programs:** her remaining respite balance follows her to the new GUIDE program.

**A safety note on respite.** A new aide or a new setting is a change in routine, and changes in routine are when people with dementia are most likely to fall or wander. Before the first respite visit, walk the aide through her home: where she gets up at night, which doors she tries, what helps her calm down, and how she moves best between bed, chair and toilet.

## How to enroll, and what to ask first

You enroll directly with a GUIDE participant that serves your ZIP code. There is no application to Medicare itself.

1. **Confirm her coverage.** Check her card or call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227). If she has a Medicare Advantage plan, she cannot join GUIDE unless she changes to Original Medicare. That is a larger decision about doctors, drug coverage and supplemental insurance, so talk it through first with your state's free State Health Insurance Assistance Program counselor (called SHINE in Florida).
2. **Find programs near you.** Use the CMS [GUIDE participant list](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/guide-participant-list.xlsx) and the CMS [participant map](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/where-innovation-happening#model=guiding-an-improved-dementia-experience-guide-model). Also ask her neurologist, primary care office or a regional Memory Disorder Clinic which GUIDE programs they work with.
3. **Call two or three programs** and ask the questions below.
4. **Give consent and complete the assessment.** The program visits the home, scores dementia stage and caregiver strain, and submits the results to CMS.
5. **Wait for confirmation.** CMS issues a final decision within 15 business days, and the program must send you a written notice within 45 days after that. If CMS says no, the letter states the reason, and you can ask to be re-evaluated if things change.

### Questions to ask any GUIDE program or referral service

- Are you the CMS-listed GUIDE participant, or a partner of one? If a partner, which participant, and what is its GUIDE ID?
- Is our ZIP code in your service area?
- Who will our care navigator be, and will we meet them in person or only by phone and video?
- Which respite settings do you offer here (in-home, adult day, overnight facility), and which local agencies provide them?
- How do we reach the 24/7 line, and who answers it?
- How will you share information with her current doctors and pharmacy?
- Will we ever be asked to pay anything? (The answer should be no for GUIDE services.)
- If we download an app or create an account, what personal and health data do you collect and share?

## Every way to get paid, reimbursed or relieved

Original Medicare does not pay family members to provide care. Money for family caregivers comes from four places: the VA, Medicaid, state aging programs, and the tax code. Medicare's role is respite (GUIDE and hospice) and paid caregiver training. The Medicaid and state tables below use Florida as the worked example; the next section shows what changes in other states.

### Medicare

| Program | What it provides | Who qualifies | Pays a family caregiver? | Start here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [GUIDE respite](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/guide) | Up to $2,625 a year of in-home, adult day or facility respite, no cost to the family | Moderate or severe dementia, unpaid caregiver, private home, enrolled in GUIDE | No; pays respite providers | A GUIDE program in your ZIP code |
| Hospice inpatient respite | Up to 5 days at a time in a Medicare-approved facility, on an occasional basis; the patient may owe 5% of the approved amount | Enrolled in the Medicare hospice benefit (which ends GUIDE enrollment) | No | Her hospice team |
| [Caregiver training services](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/health-related-social-needs-faq.pdf) | Medicare pays her doctor, nurse practitioner, mental health clinician, or physical, occupational or speech therapist to train you in daily care, safe movement and behavior management; she must consent, and sessions can be done by telehealth | Training must support her treatment plan; billed to her Part B, so usual cost sharing applies outside GUIDE | No; pays the clinician who trains you | Ask her doctor or therapist for caregiver training |

Caregiver training is the most overlooked benefit on this list. A physical or occupational therapist can teach you safe transfers, how to help her up after a fall without hurting either of you, and how to set up the bathroom and bedroom so she can do more for herself.

### Veterans Affairs

| Program | What it provides | Who qualifies | Pays a family caregiver? | Start here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers (PCAFC)](https://www.va.gov/family-and-caregiver-benefits/health-and-disability/comprehensive-assistance-for-family-caregivers/) | Monthly stipend to the Primary Family Caregiver: 62.5% (Level 1) or 100% (Level 2) of the federal GS-4, Step 1 annual pay for her area, divided by 12; plus at least 30 days of respite a year, training, counseling and health coverage if uninsured | Veteran with a service-connected rating of 70% or more who needs in-person personal care for at least 6 months | Yes, stipend | VA Caregiver Support Line 1-855-260-3274 |
| Program of General Caregiver Support Services | Training, coaching, peer support and help finding services | Caregivers of any veteran enrolled in VA health care | No | Caregiver Support Coordinator at her VA medical center |
| Veteran-Directed Care | A flexible monthly budget the veteran or family manages to hire their own aides, which can include a family member or neighbor | Enrolled in VA health care, needs help with daily activities, offered where the local VA medical center runs it | Yes, as a hired aide | VA social worker |
| [Pension with Aid and Attendance](https://www.va.gov/pension/veterans-pension-rates/) | Cash benefit: VA pays the difference between countable income and a cap of $29,093 a year for a single veteran or $34,488 with one dependent (Dec. 1, 2025 to Nov. 30, 2026). Net worth limit $163,699 | Wartime veteran or surviving spouse with limited income who needs help with daily activities | Indirectly: the cash can pay a caregiver, and care costs reduce countable income | An accredited Veterans Service Officer (free) |
| [VA respite care](https://www.va.gov/geriatrics/pages/Respite_Care.asp) | In-home, adult day or VA nursing home respite, up to 30 days per calendar year | Veteran enrolled in VA health care who needs a caregiver | No | VA social worker |

### Florida Medicaid

| Program | What it provides | Who qualifies | Pays a family caregiver? | Start here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care, Participant Direction Option](https://floridahealthjustice.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/kyr_particpant_directed_care_pdf.pdf) | She hires, schedules and supervises her own workers for companion, homemaker, attendant, personal care and nursing services; the plan sets the pay rate | Medicaid eligible (income up to 300% of the SSI benefit rate or through an income trust, plus asset limits), nursing home level of care by CARES assessment, age 65+ or disabled adult, living in her own or a family member's home | Yes, as a hired worker; confirm with the plan's case manager whether a spouse may be hired | Aging and Disability Resource Center screening via 1-800-96-ELDER |

Enrollment is not immediate. Applicants are screened, given a frailty-based priority score and placed on a statewide list. A 2026 law ([CS/HB 1121](https://flsenate.gov/Committees/BillSummaries/2026/html/1121), effective July 1, 2026) renamed it a "pre-enrollment list" and moved release screening to the local Aging and Disability Resource Centers. Screen early, and ask for a rescreening when her condition worsens, including after a fall or a hospital stay.

### Taxes, insurance and private agreements

| Option | What it does | Key limits |
|---|---|---|
| Child and Dependent Care Credit | Starting with tax year 2026, credits 20% to 50% of up to $3,000 in care costs for one qualifying person ($6,000 for two or more) | You must pay for care so you can work, and the person must be unable to care for themselves; the top rate goes to lower incomes |
| Dependent care FSA | Pay care costs with pre-tax wages, up to $7,500 per household in 2026 ($3,750 married filing separately) | Employer must offer it; the same dollars cannot also be used for the credit |
| Medical expense deduction | Qualifying care costs above 7.5% of adjusted gross income can be itemized | Ask a tax professional what qualifies |
| Long-term care insurance | Pays care costs once benefit triggers are met, usually help with 2 or more daily activities or a cognitive impairment such as dementia | Some policies pay cash or allow family caregivers; most require licensed providers. Read the policy and file the claim early |
| Personal care agreement | A written contract in which she pays a family member a fair rate for care | Needed to keep payments from being treated as gifts if she later applies for Medicaid; have an elder law attorney draft it |
| [Credit for Caring Act](https://www.collins.senate.gov/newsroom/collins-bipartisan-group-introduce-bill-to-provide-financial-relief-to-caregivers) (H.R. 2036 / S. 925) | Would create a nonrefundable credit of up to $5,000 for working family caregivers | Proposed only; not law as of this writing |

## What changes from state to state

GUIDE, VA benefits, Medicare caregiver training, hospice respite and federal tax breaks work the same in every state. What changes is Medicaid's willingness to pay family members, state tax credits, paid family leave, and the names and rules of state-funded aging programs.

### The same in every state

- **GUIDE:** identical federal rules and the same $2,625 respite cap. Only availability differs, because each program serves its own ZIP codes.
- **VA programs:** PCAFC, Veteran-Directed Care (where the local VA medical center runs it), Aid and Attendance and VA respite follow federal rules.
- **Federal tax:** the Child and Dependent Care Credit, dependent care FSA and medical expense deduction.
- **National Family Caregiver Support Program:** funded in every state through the local Area Agency on Aging, though each agency sets its own services.

### What varies

| Topic | What varies | Where things stand | How to check your state |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medicaid paying relatives | Who can be hired through self-directed home care, and whether a spouse can be paid | In KFF's 2025 survey, every responding state paid family or friends through at least one waiver. 44 states allowed waiver payments to spouses and other legally responsible relatives, usually only for "extraordinary care" beyond ordinary family help; only 6 allowed it through the regular state plan. Alaska reported no self-direction option | Ask the state Medicaid agency or Aging and Disability Resource Center which waiver serves older adults and whether a spouse may be hired |
| Structured family caregiving | A daily stipend paid through an agency to a live-in family caregiver, with nurse and coach oversight | KFF reports it for older adults and people with disabilities in Connecticut, Georgia, Indiana, Louisiana, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Rhode Island and South Dakota; for Alzheimer's in Missouri; and as standalone programs in Massachusetts and Nevada (Nevada's for dementia caregivers) | Ask the state Medicaid agency; some states exclude spouses |
| Waiting lists | Whether you can enroll right away in Medicaid home care | Many waivers cap enrollment and keep waiting or priority lists, as Florida does | Get screened early and ask about priority rules |
| State caregiver tax credits | Whether family caregivers get a state credit for out-of-pocket costs | Nebraska: 50% of eligible costs, up to $2,000, or $3,000 if the person is a veteran or has dementia; income under $50,000 ($100,000 joint); apply first, annual cap applies. Oklahoma: expanded by a 2026 law so all filers can claim up to $3,000, starting with 2026 returns. A few other states have smaller or narrower credits | Your state department of revenue |
| Paid family leave | Partial wages while you take time off to care for a family member | Programs exist in California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Maine, Maryland (benefits start 2028), Massachusetts, Minnesota, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Washington and the District of Columbia. Elsewhere, eligible workers have unpaid, job-protected federal FMLA leave | Your state labor department or employer HR |
| State-funded aging programs | Names, eligibility and waiting lists for non-Medicaid help such as respite, adult day care and caregiver subsidies | Florida's Home Care for the Elderly, Alzheimer's Disease Initiative and Community Care for the Elderly are Florida programs; other states run their own versions under different names | The [Eldercare Locator](https://eldercare.acl.gov/home) (1-800-677-1116) connects you to your local Area Agency on Aging |

One caution for every state: the 2025 federal budget law reduces federal Medicaid spending, and KFF notes that most home care programs are optional for states. Some states may trim waiver slots or family caregiver payments in coming years, so confirm current rules before you plan around them.

## Action plan: which programs to check first

Most families qualify for more than one program, and many can be combined. Start with the row that matches her situation, then work down the list.

| If she... | Check first | Then check |
|---|---|---|
| Has dementia and Original Medicare, lives at home | GUIDE | Alzheimer's Disease Initiative respite, Community Care for the Elderly, National Family Caregiver Support Program |
| Has a Medicare Advantage plan | Her plan's own caregiver and respite benefits; SHINE counseling before any switch | Alzheimer's Disease Initiative respite, National Family Caregiver Support Program |
| Has low income and few assets | Medicaid long-term care screening now, because of the pre-enrollment list | Home Care for the Elderly; GUIDE if she also has Original Medicare |
| Lives with you and you want to be paid | Medicaid Participant Direction Option (if eligible); Home Care for the Elderly subsidy | Personal care agreement drafted by an elder law attorney |
| Is a veteran or a veteran's surviving spouse | VA Caregiver Support Line; Aid and Attendance through a Veterans Service Officer | Veteran-Directed Care; PCAFC if service-connected rating is 70% or more |
| Has long-term care insurance | Her policy's benefit triggers and whether it pays family or cash | GUIDE and state programs for the gaps |
| Is in assisted living | Whether her facility is a CMS-approved GUIDE partner (no respite in this tier) | Medicaid long-term care, which can cover assisted living services |
| Is entering hospice | Hospice respite (up to 5 days at a time) | National Family Caregiver Support Program for the caregiver |
| You work and pay for her care | Child and Dependent Care Credit or dependent care FSA | Medical expense deduction |

Outside Florida, replace the Florida program names with your state's versions, and add any state caregiver tax credit or paid family leave program from the state-by-state section.

### This week

1. Call the Elder Helpline (1-800-96-ELDER) and ask for a screening for every program she may qualify for.
2. Open the CMS GUIDE participant list and call the programs that serve your ZIP code.
3. If she is a veteran or surviving spouse, call the VA Caregiver Support Line.
4. Gather her Medicare card, insurance cards, diagnosis paperwork, medication list and income and asset statements.
5. Ask her doctor or therapist to schedule caregiver training.

## Where home safety fits in

Several of these programs pay for the same things that keep a person with dementia safe at home:

- **GUIDE** requires a home visit as part of the first assessment.
- **Home Care for the Elderly** special subsidies can cover ramps, accessibility modifications and assistive devices.
- **The National Family Caregiver Support Program** can fund limited one-time home modifications and supplies.
- **Medicare caregiver training** lets a therapist teach you safe transfers and daily care routines in your own home.

When a care navigator or case manager visits, ask them to write any safety need (grab bars, door alarms, lighting, a ramp, a shower chair) into her care plan. **A need that is documented in a care plan is easier to get funded.** It also helps to bring a written list of near-misses: times she almost fell, got lost, left the stove on or tried to leave at night. Specific events carry more weight than "she's getting worse."

A professional home safety assessment can give you that list in writing, organized by room and by priority, so you walk into the care planning meeting with a plan instead of a worry.
